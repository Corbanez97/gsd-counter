# From Stencils to a New 2D Fracton Model: A Working Summary

Companion notes to *"From Stencils to Group Rings"*, compiling the applied discussion
that followed: designing a new stencil, building its Hamiltonian, testing charge
mobility, and measuring the fractal dimension of its subsystem symmetries.

---

## 1. What "number-theoretic" means (UV/IR mixing)

In the primer, $N_c(L_x,L_y)$ — the ground-state degeneracy exponent — depends on
**arithmetic** properties of $L_x, L_y$ (2-adic valuation, multiplicative order of 2
mod the odd part) rather than smooth/geometric ones. Two nearby lattice sizes can have
wildly different degeneracies:

$$N_c(256,256) = 511 \qquad N_c(256,255) = 2$$

There is no coarse-grained field theory that reproduces this — the UV detail (is $L$
divisible by 3? what power of 2 divides it?) directly controls the IR observable
(ground-state degeneracy). This is the defining signature of **UV/IR mixing**, and it
contrasts with the toric code, where GSD = 4 regardless of lattice size.

---

## 2. Designing a new fracton stencil (inverse problem)

**Recipe.** A stencil $f(x,y)=\sum_k g_k(x)y^k$ is equivalent to a linear recurrence
over $\mathbb{F}_2[x]$ in the "row index" $y$. To go from a *desired* recursion to a
stencil:

1. Choose $g_0(x), \dots, g_{n-1}(x)$ freely (set leading coefficient $g_n=1$).
2. The stencil is $f(x,y) = y^n + g_{n-1}(x)y^{n-1} + \cdots + g_0(x)$.
3. $A$ = "multiply by $f$" in $\mathbb{F}_2[x,y]/(x^{L_x}-1,y^{L_y}-1)$ — same
   convolution-matrix construction as `nc_sweep.py`.
4. $N_c = \dim\ker A$; genuine fractal behavior appears whenever $f$ depends
   nontrivially on both $x$ and $y$ (no cross-term $xy$ required — Newman–Moore's
   $f=1+x+y$ already shows this).

**Worked example — the "trinomial" stencil:**

$$t_{r+1} = (1+x+x^2)\,t_r \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad f(x,y) = 1 + x + x^2 + y$$

Shifts (row, col): $(0,0), (0,1), (0,2), (1,0)$.

Sanity check on the known example first: rebuilt $A$ for haah_z on $32\times32$,
got $N_c=63$, matching the primer exactly.

**$N_c$ for the trinomial stencil** (computed via Gaussian elimination over GF(2)):

| $L_x=L_y$ | 15 | 16 | 17 | 31 | 32 | 33 | 63 | 64 | 65 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| $N_c$ | 13 | 16 | 1 | 31 | 32 | 1 | 61 | 64 | 1 |

Same UV/IR-mixing signature as haah_z: extensive growth at powers of 2, collapsing to
$N_c=1$ at $L=2^k+1$. Since this stencil has $y$ only to the first power (no nodal
point), it admits a clean closed form via the same method as §17 of the primer
(Newman–Moore family), just replacing $g_{NM}=1+x$ with $g=1+x+x^2$:

$$N_c(L_x,L_y) = \min(2^a,2^b)\cdot \deg\gcd_{\mathbb{F}_2}\!\big(y^{m_y}+1,\ g(x)^{m_x}+1\big),
\qquad L_x=2^am_x,\ L_y=2^bm_y$$

— no need to pass to $\mathbb{F}_4$, since there is no singular point to resolve.

## 3. The Hamiltonian

Reading the stencil shifts directly as eq. (6) of the primer:

$$H = -J\sum_{i,j} \sigma_{i,j}\,\sigma_{i,j+1}\,\sigma_{i,j+2}\,\sigma_{i+1,j}$$

A quartic, frustration-free classical spin model on the torus (indices mod $L_x, L_y$).

- **Ground states** = $\ker A$ (dimension $N_c$, e.g. 32 on the $32\times32$ torus).
- **Flip symmetries** = $\ker A^\top$, generated by the *reversed* stencil
  $(0,0),(0,-1),(0,-2),(-1,0)$ — same dimension as $\ker A$ (primer §8).
- These symmetry generators are fractal-shaped, non-deformable operators (verified by
  extracting an actual kernel basis vector and rendering it — it shows nested,
  self-similar triangular structure, directly analogous to Fig. 2c/2d).

## 4. Restricted mobility of charges

Since flipping a spin pattern $u$ changes the defect (syndrome) pattern by exactly
$Au$, mobility questions become concrete linear-algebra checks against the same
matrix that defines the ground states.

**Test 1 — straight-string operator.**

| Model | horizontal string of 8 flips |
|---|---|
| Control, $f=1+x$ (ordinary domain wall) | clean 2 endpoint defects → **mobile**, like a toric-code anyon |
| Our quartic model, $f=1+x+x^2+y$ | 16 scattered defects along the interior (horizontal), 18 (vertical) → **no clean transport** |

**Test 2 — elementary excitation shape.** A single spin flip does not create one
defect; it creates a rigid 4-point cluster at fixed relative offsets (the reversed
stencil shape stamped down at the flipped site), e.g. flipping $(10,10)$ produces
defects at $(10,10),(10,11),(10,12),(11,10)$.

**Test 3 — individual defect hopping.** Exhaustive search over all local flip
combinations (weight 1–2, $7\times7$ neighborhood) attempting to hop *one* of those
four bound defects by one lattice site while leaving the other three and everything
else untouched: **no solution exists.** The point is permanently bound to its
partners at this range.

**Conclusion.** This is genuine restricted mobility — the defining feature separating
type-I fracton order (Newman–Moore, haah_z, and this quartic model) from ordinary 2D
topological order. Charges cannot move via any string operator; only the same
fractal-shaped combinations that generate the symmetry group can act non-trivially
across the lattice.

## 5. Fractal dimension of the subsystem symmetry

Question: is the symmetry generator itself a "1D" fractal (dimension between 1 and 2,
sparse) or does it fill area (dimension 2)?

**Theory.** In characteristic 2, $g(x)^{2^n} = g(x^{2^n})$ (Frobenius), so
$g(x)^r = \prod_i g(x^{2^i})$ over the set bits of $r$. If $g$ has $T$ terms with *no
cancellation*, row $r$ has $T^{\text{popcount}(r)}$ nonzero entries, and total filled
area over $N=2^n$ rows is $(1+T)^n = N^{\log_2(1+T)}$ — that exponent is the
box-counting dimension.

- Newman–Moore ($g=1+x$, $T=2$): predicts $\log_2 3 \approx 1.585$ — matches the
  classic Sierpiński gasket exactly, confirmed numerically to 4 decimal places.
- Trinomial ($g=1+x+x^2$, $T=3$): naive "no cancellation" prediction is
  $\log_2 4 = 2$ (space-filling) — **this turned out to be wrong.**

**Numerical fit** (total filled cells vs. $N=2^n$ rows, $n=4..12$, log-log slope):

| Model | fitted dimension |
|---|---|
| Newman–Moore | 1.5850 |
| Trinomial | **1.6945** |

**Why the naive prediction failed:** $g=1+x+x^2$ has exponents $\{0,1,2\}$, which are
not clean, non-colliding binary digits the way Newman–Moore's $\{0,1\}$ are. Under
Frobenius rescaling across different bit positions, the exponent-2 term overlaps and
cancels (mod 2) against exponent-0/1 terms from neighboring scales, so the true growth
is well below the marginal value of 2.

**Two distinctions worth keeping separate:**
1. The *number* of independent symmetry generators grows only linearly in $L$
   (sub-extensive) — a statement about $N_c(L)$.
2. The *spatial support* of a single generator has fractal (box-counting) dimension
   $\approx 1.69$ — a statement about that one generator's shape.

Both point toward non-trivial, sub-2D structure, but are logically independent
quantities that must be checked separately for any new stencil.

**Open thread:** an exact (non-fitted) value of this dimension can be obtained via
Willson's theorem for GF(2)-linear CA fractals — the largest eigenvalue of a transfer
matrix built from $g(x) \bmod (x^k-1)$'s coefficient-collision pattern, for a window
size $k$ covering $g$'s support.

## 6. Open directions flagged along the way

- Add a cross term (e.g. $f=1+x+x^2+y+xy$) to force a genuine nodal singularity in
  this family, making it structurally closer to haah_z's Theorem 13.1 rather than the
  Newman–Moore family.
- Derive the closed form for the trinomial stencil in full, and locate the analog of
  the "$3\mid\gcd(m_x,m_y)$" criterion for anomalously large degeneracy.
- Check whether *fractal-shaped* (non-straight) operators, following the same
  branching pattern as the kernel vectors, can transport charges cleanly over longer
  distances — i.e. classify mobility exactly rather than just ruling out straight
  strings.
- Compare to Haah's actual 3D cubic code, where isolated point-like fractons appear
  natively in 3D, versus this 2D model's sub-dimensional excitations.
- Compute the trinomial stencil's fractal dimension exactly via Willson's
  transfer-matrix method rather than a numerical fit.
